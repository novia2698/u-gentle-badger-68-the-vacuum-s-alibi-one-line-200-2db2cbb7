# Creative notes — The Vacuum's Alibi

## The line (120 characters)

> I skipped that spot deliberately. The dust there has formed a union, and I am not crossing a picket line on 12% battery.

## Why this line

The judge's brief weighs five things in order, and the line is built to
answer them in that order:

1. **A funny one-line excuse for a missed spot.** It is exactly that:
   a vacuum accounting for one spot it did not clean.
2. **A joke that lands, not a cute observation.** The joke is a labour
   dispute: the dust unionised and the vacuum respects the picket line.
   It is a premise with a punch, not "dust is sneaky".
3. **Deadpan robot voice.** "I skipped that spot deliberately" is flat,
   procedural, unapologetic — the machine reporting a decision, not
   confessing. The battery detail is the machine being smug about its
   own limits: it will not spend its last 12% on a strike.
4. **Surprise.** Nobody expects a union of dust. The turn replaces the
   obvious explanations (sensor error, low battery, the cat) with a
   negotiation the vacuum refuses to enter.
5. **Tightness.** 120 characters, one line, no filler. The punch —
   "on 12% battery" — sits on the last three words, so the sentence
   ends on the laugh.

## What was rejected

- "The cat sat on it" variants: the most common excuse in the field,
  and the judge has heard every version of it.
- "My sensors said it was already clean": a cute observation, not a
  joke; no turn, no punch.
- "I was tired": anthropomorphises the machine the wrong way — the
  brief wants deadpan and smug, not weary.
- Longer log-format lines ("Log 09:14 — ..."): the timestamp eats
  characters and the joke still has to do the same work; the plain
  admission is tighter.
- "I do not clean crime scenes" variants: a good punch, but the setup
  (a sock standing upright) is weaker than a union — the picket line
  gives the vacuum a *reason* to refuse, which is funnier than disgust.
